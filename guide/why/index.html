How it works, and why

The reasons behind the choices in CmdGlas. For step-by-step help, use the quick start.

Nothing found. Try one simple word.

It never touches the game

CmdGlas does not read the game's memory, inject anything, hook into the game or play it for you. The tablet is a screen that reads files and web data. The Command Deck sends a normal key press, the same as your keyboard.

Why: anything that reaches inside a game can trip anti-cheat and put your account at risk. Staying outside the game keeps you safe.

The deck uses your own saved keys

The Command Deck reads the keybinds you already saved in the game and sends those. You do not type them in or remap anything.

Why: you already set your keys up the way you like them. The deck follows you, not the other way round. It never changes your game's keybind file: FREE KEYS PICKER makes a backup and writes a separate profile that you load yourself.

It never takes focus from the game

Tapping the Command Deck, the Kneeboard or the Halo does not pull the game out of the front. Only while you type in a settings box does the program borrow the keyboard, and it hands it back when you are done.

Why: if a click moved focus, your next key press would go to the wrong window in the middle of a fight. The game must keep the keyboard and mouse.

Why Borderless mode

The game must run in Borderless window mode (Settings > Graphics > Window Mode).

Why: in full screen, Windows gives the whole screen to the game and hides every other window, so nothing can show on top of it.

Key rules: one or two modifiers

Show / hide keys and free keys use Ctrl, Alt or Shift plus one key, at most two modifiers.

Why: one or two modifiers are quick to press when it counts. Three held modifiers plus a key is slow, easy to miss, and many keyboards drop one of four keys held at once.

Not allowed: a single letter on its own (it would fire while you type in chat), Alt + F4 (it closes programs), and number-pad keys with two modifiers. With Num Lock on, Windows turns Shift + a number-pad key into a different key (Shift + Num 4 becomes the Left arrow), so those combinations are not reliable.

No look-alike keys

The game can still see the keys you press. If a CmdGlas key is the same as, or very close to, a game key, one press can do two things.

That is why there are two helpers: Key check reads your game keys and shows every clash, and FREE KEYS PICKER only picks combinations that nothing uses, not your saved keys and not the game's standard keys.

Protected buttons

SELF DESTRUCT and EJECT live in the EMERGENCY drawer and must be held for 1.5 seconds. RESPAWN also needs a 1.5 second hold.

Why: a touch screen is easy to brush by accident. A deliberate hold means a stray finger cannot end your ship.

Your files and backups

CmdGlas never changes your game files unless you press a button and confirm it. A game backup only reads your files and checks every copy. A restore asks first, refuses while the game runs, and keeps an undo copy.

Why: your keybinds and characters took you hours. Nothing should be able to lose them by surprise.

Privacy

  • No AI is downloaded or installed with any CmdGlas product.
  • The EDGE Link only listens on your own PC (127.0.0.1) and sends nothing over the internet.
  • A license key is checked with the store when you activate it, then at least once every 7 days. That is how a key works on 2 PCs and can be moved.
  • A troubleshooting report leaves out your Windows user name.

Honest numbers

When a data source cannot be reached, the tablet says so. It never fills the gap with a guess. Test-version numbers are marked as test numbers.

Why: a wrong number you trust is worse than no number. The threat check is a quick sanity check, not a promise: pilot skill still decides most fights.

Free tablet, paid programs

The tablet is free in your browser. The Windows programs are paid, and every one of them comes with the CmdGlas Tablet + Halo.

Why: the programs pay for hosting and for the hours behind every update, so the tablet can stay free for everyone.
